Getting your hand histories

  1. In the PlayGG client, open PokerCraft.
  2. Find your cash game sessions and use the option to download their hand histories.
  3. Upload the .zip here as it is. No need to unzip it.

Hands you've already added are skipped automatically, so it's safe to upload overlapping files.

GG files often show opponents as 8-character codes instead of screen names. Open a player and add the name you see at the table. The sessions count on each player shows whether the same code comes back in later imports.

Use it after you play

In October 2026 GGPoker tightened its rules on third-party software, and tracking tools are named in the change. This page never connects to the poker client, but check PlayGG's current terms before you rely on it, and keep it closed while your tables are open.

Replay a hand

Paste one hand from a hand history. Each of your decisions is checked against the odds using only what you knew at that moment: your cards, the board, the price and each opponent's likely range. Opponents you've tracked for 30+ hands get ranges from their real stats.

How the numbers are worked out

  1. Hand strength. Every 5-to-7-card hand gets one score: its category (pair, flush…) times 135, plus its kickers in base 13. Comparing two hands is then comparing two numbers.
  2. Equity is your share of the pot if all the cards were dealt out with no more betting. It's estimated by Monte Carlo simulation: deal each opponent a hand from their range (skipping hands that clash with cards already seen), deal the rest of the board at random, and count wins and split pots. 30,000 simulated hands give an error of about ±0.3 points. Results were checked against exact enumeration of every runout; for example JTs vs 22 comes out at 54.0% both ways.
  3. Ranges. The 169 starting hands are ranked by their equity against one random hand. A “top 25%” range takes the best hands until it holds 25% of the 1,326 possible two-card combinations. For a tracked player, a caller gets their VPIP as the range width, a raiser their PFR and a 3-bettor their 3-bet percentage.
  4. Pot odds. The equity you need to call is to call ÷ (pot + to call), where the pot already includes the bet you face.
  5. Expected value of calling, compared with folding: EV = equity × (pot + to call) − to call. Positive means calling makes money in the long run.
  6. Narrowing after the flop. When an opponent bets, their range keeps only hands that use a hole card to make a pair or better, flush or straight draws, and about 30% of the rest as bluffs. When they raise, it keeps two pair or better, top pair or an overpair, strong draws and about 10% bluffs. Each bet or raise in the hand narrows the range again on that street's board.
  7. Limits. Equity assumes the hand is checked down from here. It ignores money won or lost on later streets (implied odds), the chance your bet makes them fold (fold equity), position and rake. The narrowing rules are a simple model of real ranges, not a solver. Treat close spots (within 3 points) as judgment calls.
